SKILLPROB AI - RESUME-BASED INTERVIEW PRACTICE PLATFORM

1. PROJECT OVERVIEW

SkillProb AI is a web-based interview practice platform. A candidate can upload
a resume, select an interview domain and experience level, and answer AI-generated
interview questions using typed or spoken responses.

The platform evaluates every answer, provides a suggested improved answer, and
shows a final performance report. If the candidate enables the camera, the browser
also produces local, privacy-aware visibility and attention signals. No video is
recorded or uploaded.


2. MAIN OBJECTIVE

The main objective is to help candidates practise technical and behavioural
interviews with structured, personalised feedback.

The system can:

- Accept resume text and supported resume files.
- Generate interview questions based on the selected domain and experience level.
- Accept typed and speech-recognised answers.
- Score each answer for grammar, relevance, confidence, and STAR-method structure.
- Give feedback and a better-answer suggestion after every question.
- Ask a follow-up question when an answer is too short, vague, or lacks depth.
- Provide optional local camera feedback about face visibility, eye visibility,
  gaze near the screen, multiple faces, and poor lighting.
- Produce a final interview feedback page and report.

4. RESUME INPUT

The candidate may provide resume information by uploading a file or entering
resume text. Supported upload formats are:

- PDF
- TXT
- DOCX

The extracted text is saved for the current interview session so the question
generator can use it for interview context.


5. INTERVIEW SETUP

Before an interview begins, the candidate selects:

- Domain, such as a technical area or job focus.
- Experience level, such as beginner, intermediate, or experienced.

These choices guide the difficulty and context of generated questions.


6. AI-GENERATED QUESTIONS

The platform uses Google Gemini when a GEMINI_API_KEY is configured. Gemini can
generate questions using the resume context, selected domain, and experience level.

If Gemini is unavailable, the application uses local fallback questions so the
interview can still continue.

Questions may be technical, behavioural, situational, or project-focused.


7. ANSWER SUBMISSION

Candidates can answer in either of two ways:

- Type directly in the answer area.
- Use browser speech recognition to speak the answer.

When both typed text and a speech transcript are present, the system combines them
without duplicating the same content.


8. ANSWER EVALUATION

Each submitted answer receives four scores out of 10:

Grammar
    Measures sentence clarity, structure, completion, and the effect of filler words.

Relevance
    Measures whether the response directly addresses the question and its key topic.

Confidence
    Estimates how clearly and completely the candidate communicates. It considers
    answer detail, sentence structure, relevance, sentiment, and filler words.

STAR Method
    Measures whether a behavioural answer includes Situation, Task, Action, and Result.

A higher score indicates a stronger answer in that area. The scores are practice
feedback, not an official hiring decision.


9. FEEDBACK AFTER EVERY QUESTION

After every answer, the candidate sees:

- Grammar, relevance, confidence, and STAR-method scores.
- Filler-word count.
- Personalised AI feedback.
- A suggested better answer when available.
- A follow-up question when more explanation is needed.
- An optional camera-check summary and practical suggestions for that answer.


10. FOLLOW-UP QUESTIONS

The platform can ask an additional question when the answer is:

- Short (generally fewer than 30 words).
- Vague or unclear.
- Missing depth or a concrete example.

Example:

Question:
"Describe a difficult project you worked on."

Candidate:
"I made a web application with my team."

Possible follow-up:
"What was your specific responsibility, what challenge did you solve, and what
was the result?"


11. CAMERA CHECK

The camera check is optional and runs in the browser only after the candidate gives
camera permission. It analyses aggregate signals; it does not record or upload video.

Signals shown after each answer can include:

- Samples with no face detected.
- Samples with more than one face.
- Samples looking away from the screen.
- Samples where eyes were not clearly visible.
- Low-light or possible lens-obstruction samples.

The platform presents these as coaching or review signals. They are not proof of
misconduct and must not be treated as an automatic decision.


12. MICROPHONE AND SPEECH RECOGNITION

The microphone is optional. When enabled, browser speech recognition can create a
transcript for the candidate's answer. The platform also counts common filler words,
such as "um", "uh", "like", and "you know", in the submitted transcript.


13. FINAL PERFORMANCE FEEDBACK

At the end of the interview, the feedback page shows:

- Average grammar, relevance, confidence, and STAR-method scores.
- Question-by-question feedback.
- Submitted answers and answer duration.
- Filler-word counts.
- The camera-check summary for the complete interview, if camera data was collected.


14. TECHNOLOGY STACK

Frontend:

- HTML and Jinja templates
- CSS
- JavaScript
- Tailwind CSS (CDN)
- Font Awesome icons

Backend:

- Python
- Flask
- Flask-Session

AI and language processing:

- Google Gemini API (optional)
- TextBlob sentiment analysis
- NLTK

Browser features:

- Web Speech API for speech recognition
- MediaPipe Face Landmarker for local camera checks
- getUserMedia for camera and microphone access

Data and files:

- SQLite database

    Handles optional camera/microphone controls and local camera-check signals.


16. IMPORTANT PRIVACY PRINCIPLE

Camera feedback is designed for interview practice. It reports only aggregate,
observable signals and does not store or upload webcam video. A signal such as
"looking away" or "more than one face" is not proof of cheating or misconduct.
Human judgement is always required.
